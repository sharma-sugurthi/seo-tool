Ask a controller how they found their last tool and the answer is rarely "a Google ad". It is a Reddit thread in r/Accounting, a peer in a Slack community, a "best X tools" list, or, increasingly, a question typed into ChatGPT or answered by Google's AI Overview. This post walks through what those channels cite and what a software company can do to show up.

## What AI answers are built from

Run a handful of buyer questions ("best AP automation for NetSuite", "AI bookkeeping for startups") through ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google and look at the sources. The pattern is consistent:

1. **Listicles** on niche publications and review sites. Being in the list matters more than ranking first.
2. **Reddit and community threads** where practitioners name tools and explain why. These get cited heavily because they read as unbiased.
3. **Review platforms** such as G2 and Capterra, for ratings and feature comparisons.
4. **The vendor's own documentation** for feature specifics, pricing and integrations.
5. **Expert articles** with named authors and concrete numbers.

Paid ads, homepages and generic blog posts almost never appear.

## What that means for a vendor

- **Get listed everywhere relevant.** Directories and listicles are the cheapest citations you will ever earn. Submit to the AI and SaaS directories, then pitch every "best tools" article in your category for inclusion.
- **Show up on Reddit honestly.** Have a real person from the team answer questions in the subreddits your buyers use. Mention the product when it is the genuine answer and disclose who you are. Threads like that get cited for years.
- **Publish practitioner content.** Articles with a named author, screenshots and numbers get cited. Thin SEO filler does not.
- **Keep documentation and pricing pages clear.** AI systems quote them directly.
- **Founders on LinkedIn.** Not a citation source for AI, but where accountants and CFOs decide who they trust.

## A 90 day plan

| Weeks | Focus | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Audit | Which questions mention you, which cite competitors, which sources they use |
| 3 to 6 | Directories and listicles | 100 plus submissions, 10 listicle pitches, review profiles claimed |
| 5 to 10 | Reddit and community | Weekly helpful answers in 4 to 6 subreddits and Slack groups |
| 6 to 12 | Content and LinkedIn | 2 expert articles a month, 3 founder posts a week |
| 12 | Re audit | Compare citation counts and sources against week 1 |
