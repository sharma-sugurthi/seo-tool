Most firms already have staff using ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini to draft emails, summarise tax guidance and write formulas. Few have written down the rules. That gap is where the risk sits: not the tool, but the absence of a policy. Here is a short one you can adopt this week, with the reasoning behind each rule.

## The three questions that decide everything

1. **Who can see what I paste?** Consumer plans may use conversations to train models unless you turn that off. Business plans generally do not. Know which one your staff are logged into.
2. **Is this data identifiable?** A client's name, tax ID, bank details or employee list is identifiable. A column of anonymised amounts is not.
3. **Would I be comfortable if the client saw this prompt?** If not, do not send it.

## The policy

**Allowed without asking**

- Drafting emails, engagement letters and internal memos with no client identifiers
- Explaining accounting standards, tax rules and software features
- Writing and debugging spreadsheet formulas and scripts on sample data
- Summarising publicly available documents

**Allowed on a business plan with training turned off**

- Summarising client documents after removing names, IDs and account numbers
- Categorisation suggestions on exported transactions with counterparties anonymised
- First drafts of management commentary using aggregate figures

**Never**

- Uploading full ledgers, bank statements, payroll files or tax returns with identifiers intact
- Pasting login credentials, MFA codes or API keys
- Using AI output as a final answer on a tax position without a human review and a source
- Using a personal account for any client work

## Settings to check today

| Tool | Setting |
|---|---|
| ChatGPT | Team or Enterprise plan, or Data Controls with training off |
| Claude | Team or Enterprise plan; review data retention settings |
| Gemini | Workspace edition with admin controls, not a personal Google account |
| Microsoft Copilot | Commercial data protection enabled through your Microsoft 365 tenant |

## Make it practical

- Put a one line reminder in the tools staff already use: "No client identifiers in AI prompts."
- Keep a shared prompt library for approved uses. Good prompts spread good habits.
- Review the policy every six months. The tools change faster than that.
- Tell clients. A short paragraph in your engagement letter about how you use AI builds trust rather than eroding it.

## Where AI tools built for finance fit in

Purpose built tools such as [Karbon](/tools/karbon/), [Digits](/tools/digits/) and [Intuit Assist](/tools/quickbooks-intuit-assist/) run AI inside systems that already hold client data under a contract you have signed. That is a different risk profile from pasting data into a general chatbot, and it is usually the better path for anything touching real ledgers.
